Case Study: Canva and Bevel

Read each case snapshot and use the linked source when directed by your teacher. Record what the evidence supports and identify what remains unknown.

Melanie Perkins – Canva

While teaching design, Melanie Perkins noticed that existing design software was difficult for many users to learn. She began with a focused yearbook product that allowed the team to understand the problem and improve the experience before expanding the larger idea.

Reading focus: What frustrated users, and how did a focused first product help Melanie learn?

📖Click here to read the Bevel Case StudyOpens in a new tab ↗

Tristan Walker – Bevel

Tristan Walker focused on grooming needs that many mainstream products did not address well for Black men. The opportunity began with an underserved customer and expanded as the company continued to respond to that customer's needs.

Reading focus: Whose needs was Bevel addressing, and how did the founder explain expanding the line?

Sample Answer (Different Founder) — Example Only

This example uses a different founder so you still do the Canva and Bevel thinking yourself.

Founder: Kavita Shukla – FreshPaper

Frustration: Families threw away fruit and vegetables that spoiled too quickly.

Empathy, not assumption: She tested her spice-paper idea on real produce and listened to farmers and food banks about what they needed.

Still to learn: Whether customers would pay for it and remember to use it every week.

SCAMPER Solution Studio

Return to Tuesday's pain point. Generate options before choosing. Use at least five ideas and at least three different SCAMPER moves.

Sample Answer — Example Only

Tuesday's user and pain point: Seventh graders lose warm-up time waiting at the one pencil sharpener.

SCAMPER MoveSolution IdeaHow It Responds to the Pain Point
SubstituteUse mechanical pencils in a class binNo sharpening needed, so no line forms
CombineAdd a sharpener to each table's supply caddySpreads the wait across four spots
ReverseSharpen pencils at the end of class, not the startMoves the wait to a time that doesn't cost warm-up

SCAMPER: Substitute · Combine · Adapt · Modify · Put to another use · Eliminate · Reverse or rearrange

SCAMPER MoveSolution IdeaHow It Responds to the Pain Point

Quick Examples

  • Chester Greenwood combined familiar materials to address one winter discomfort.
  • FreshPaper adapted familiar materials for the problem of produce spoilage.
  • Braigo substituted lower-cost components to test a more accessible approach.
