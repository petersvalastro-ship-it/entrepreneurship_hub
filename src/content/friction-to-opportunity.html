Classify Needs and Wants

🧰

Need

Something a person must have to be safe, healthy, or able to do a required task.

Without it, something important goes wrong.

Examples: water, a pencil for a test, a coat in winter.

✨

Want

Something a person would like to have because it is fun, stylish, faster, or more comfortable.

Without it, life is less enjoyable — but still works.

Examples: gel pens, a brand-name hoodie, a phone case.

⚖️

Both

The basic version meets a need; the extra features are wants.

It depends on the person and situation.

Example: a backpack (need) vs. a $90 designer backpack (want).

Quick test: Ask “What happens if this person doesn’t have it?” If the answer is “they can’t stay safe, healthy, or do the required task,” it’s a need. If it’s “they’d be less happy or comfortable,” it’s a want. Businesses succeed by meeting needs — and often earn more by adding wants on top.

Choose five products or services students use at school. Classify each one and explain the customer motivation. The same item may serve a need for one person and a want for another.

Quick Synthesis

Sample Answer — Example Only

Needs and wants are hard to separate because one product can do both jobs. A water bottle meets the need to stay hydrated, but a student may pick a $40 bottle because it looks cool — that part is a want.

Observe a School Routine

Choose a routine you can observe respectfully: entering class, organizing materials, finding an item, moving through a shared space, or completing a common task. Record only what you see or hear.

👀 What I see+👂 What I hear≠🤔 Why I think it happened

✓ Good (fact): “Six students waited in line at the pencil sharpener before class started. Two went back to their seats without sharpening.”

✗ Not yet (explanation): “Students are lazy about getting ready.”

Separate Evidence from Assumption

Observation describes what happened. Assumption is your interpretation of why it happened.

Sample Answer — Example Only

Factual observation: The line at the pencil sharpener lasted about 3 minutes, and the bell rang before everyone was done.

Possible explanation (needs testing): Maybe there is only one working sharpener for 25 students.

04

Frame the Opportunity

Use your observation evidence to define one focused problem. Do not solve it yet.

🙋 User→😣 Pain→📍 Situation→💥 Impact
Sample Answer — Example Only

Pain point: Students lose class time waiting to sharpen pencils.

Problem statement: Seventh graders experience long waits when they need a sharp pencil at the start of class, which causes them to miss the warm-up.

Possible root cause (hypothesis): I think there aren’t enough working sharpeners for the number of students.

How might we… help students get class-ready supplies before the bell without waiting in line?
